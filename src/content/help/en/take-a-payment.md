Most payments happen at the desk while the patient is standing there, so this needs to take seconds.

## Where to find it

Press **Quick Pay** on the dashboard. The screen is titled **Receive Payment**.

![The Receive Payment screen](/help/en/quick-pay.png)

## Step 1 — Find the patient

Search by **name or phone number**. The system then reads their account and lists everything they still owe.

## Step 2 — Choose what they're paying

Two choices, and they behave differently.

### Paying a specific bill

Under **Outstanding Procedures** you'll see each unpaid treatment with **Owes:** and the remaining amount. Pick the one they're settling.

Once selected you get three numbers:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Total Cost** | What the treatment costs |
| **Paid So Far** | What's already been paid against it |
| **Amount Paying Now** | What you're taking today |

**You cannot enter more than the remaining balance.** Try it and you'll get *Payment amount cannot exceed the remaining balance*. That's deliberate — it stops a treatment from showing as overpaid.

### General Payment

Choose **General Payment** to put money on the patient's account without tying it to any one treatment. It's described in the app as *Add advance credit*.

Use it when someone pays a deposit up front, or hands you a round sum to go against whatever they owe.

**Prefer paying a specific bill when you can.** A general payment reduces the balance but doesn't tell you *which* treatment got settled — so later, when you're working out what's still outstanding on a particular crown, the trail is muddier.

## Step 3 — Confirm

Press **Confirm Payment**.

That's the moment the money becomes real: it appears in the day's takings on the dashboard, in the ledger, and against the patient's account. If the treatment had a dentist commission or a lab fee, both are worked out now — see the commissions article.

## Why the payment must be its own record

The charge and the payment are two separate entries. The treatment creates the charge; taking money creates a payment; the balance is the difference.

That's why you can't "mark a treatment as paid" — you record the payment, and the balance follows. It also means a patient paying in three instalments has three payment records, and you can see exactly when each one arrived.

## What's next

Everything about one patient's money lives in their file.
