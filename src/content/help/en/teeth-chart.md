The odontogram is where findings get recorded against individual teeth, rather than as a paragraph of notes nobody can search later.

## Where to find it

Open the patient and press **Diagnosis** in the Quick Actions row. The page is titled **Dental Diagnoses**.

![The odontogram with its tooth grid and diagnosis colours](/help/en/teeth-chart.png)

## Adult and child

The **Adult / Child** toggle at the top switches the chart between the permanent dentition and the primary one. Get this right before you start charting a child — the tooth numbers are different, and findings recorded against adult numbers on a six-year-old will not mean what you want them to mean.

Teeth are numbered **FDI**: 18–11 and 21–28 across the top, 48–41 and 31–38 across the bottom, with **RIGHT** and **LEFT** marked so there's no ambiguity about whose left.

## Charting a finding

**Tap a tooth on the chart to begin.** Then pick what you found. The categories are colour-coded and sit under the chart:

| | |
|---|---|
| Dental Caries | Pulpal Status |
| Periapical Status | Hypersensitivity |
| Tooth Wear / NCCL | Traumatic Injuries |
| Periodontal | Developmental |
| Restoration / Prosthesis | Surgical / Extracted |

Each finding you record appears in the **Diagnoses Log** on the right, and the **Affected Teeth** counter at the top goes up.

## Clinical and Perio

Two views of the same mouth, toggled above the chart:

- **Clinical** — the findings above.
- **Perio** — periodontal charting.

## Finding something later

The Diagnoses Log has a search box — **by tooth, status or notes**. This is the reason for charting on teeth rather than writing prose: six months later you can ask "what did we find on 26?" and get an answer in a second.

## Saving

The chart saves as you go. **All Saved** at the top is the confirmation — if it says anything else, wait before closing the page.

## The printed report

**PDF report** produces a diagnosis report for the patient, or to file, or to send to a specialist you're referring to.
