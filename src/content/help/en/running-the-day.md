Every appointment carries a **stage**. Keeping stages honest through the day is what makes the dashboard, the waiting room screen and the reports mean anything.

## The stages

| Stage | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Unconfirmed** | Booked, but the patient hasn't confirmed |
| **Confirmed** | They've said they're coming |
| **Check in** | They've arrived and are in the waiting room |
| **In chair** | They're being treated |
| **Check out** | Treatment finished, settling up |
| **Completed** | Done and paid |
| **Delayed** | Running late, still expected |
| **No show** | Didn't come and didn't say |
| **Canceled** | Called it off |

A normal visit runs **Unconfirmed → Confirmed → Check in → In chair → Check out → Completed**.

## Changing a stage

Click any appointment to open it, then pick the new stage under **Status**.

![An appointment open with its status picker](/help/en/appointment-details.png)

The dashboard also gives you one-click buttons on today's list — **Arrive**, **Seat**, **Check Out** — which do the same thing without opening anything. That's the fast path when the waiting room is full.

## Why "Check in" matters more than the others

Marking someone **Check in** isn't just a label. It's the moment the system starts counting how long they've been waiting, and it's what puts them on the waiting-room list.

Skip it — jump straight from Confirmed to Completed at the end of the visit — and you lose your waiting-time figures for that patient entirely. That number is the one your patients notice most.

## Waiting mood

When someone is checked in, you can tag how they're doing: **Happy**, **Neutral**, **Anxious**, or **In Pain**.

![Today's schedule — the mood tag beside a patient, and the one-click Arrive / Seat / Check Out buttons](/help/en/waiting-mood.png)

It takes one tap and it changes how the room is run. Someone marked *In Pain* who has been waiting forty minutes is a problem worth seeing before they complain, not after.

## No show vs Canceled

Worth keeping apart, because they mean different things about the patient.

- **Canceled** — they told you. Rebook them.
- **No show** — they didn't. That's the number to watch, because a patient who no-shows twice will probably do it again.

Marking every missed appointment as *Canceled* to be polite makes your no-show rate look perfect and hides a real problem.
