Plans change constantly. What matters is doing it in a way that keeps the patient informed and the record honest.

## Moving an appointment

1. Click the appointment in the calendar.
2. Press **Edit**.
3. Change the date, time, dentist or duration.
4. Save.

![The appointment details view with Edit and Delete](/help/en/appointment-edit.png)

The same two warnings from booking apply — a closed day, or a slot already taken — and, as before, they ask rather than block.

## Cancelling

Open the appointment and set its **Status** to **Canceled**.

Do this rather than deleting. A cancelled appointment stays in the record: you can see it happened, who cancelled, and when. That history is what tells you a particular patient cancels every other visit.

## Cancelling tells the patient

If WhatsApp is set up for your clinic, cancelling sends the patient a message automatically. There are three automatic messages in total:

| When | Message |
|---|---|
| Booking made | Confirmation with the date and time |
| Booking changed | The new date and time |
| Booking cancelled | Told it's off |

You don't have to do anything to trigger them — changing the appointment is enough. Which is exactly why it matters that you **cancel** rather than **delete**.

> On the free plan, WhatsApp messaging isn't included. These messages start working once your plan covers it — see the WhatsApp article for setting it up.

## Cancel vs Delete — the difference that matters

There's a **Delete** button too. The two are not the same thing.

- **Cancel** — the appointment stays, marked as cancelled. The patient is told. It shows in reports.
- **Delete** — the appointment is removed. No message goes out. No trace it ever existed.

**Cancel is almost always the right choice.** Delete is for genuine mistakes — a double booking made by accident, a test entry, a patient booked into the wrong file. Anything the patient actually knew about should be cancelled, not deleted.

Deleting an appointment the patient was expecting means they turn up to a clinic with no record of them, and nobody can explain what happened.

## If the patient just doesn't come

Don't cancel it after the fact — mark it **No show**. See the previous article for why that distinction is worth keeping.
