## Where to find it

Open the patient and press **Write Rx** in the Quick Actions row. The page is called **Prescription Studio**, and the header confirms who you're writing for.

![The Prescription Studio with its live preview](/help/en/prescription-studio.png)

## The live preview

The right-hand side shows the prescription exactly as it will print — your clinic name, address and phone across the top, taken from your clinic profile.

If that header is wrong or empty, fix it in **Settings → Clinic Profile** rather than here. It's the same header on every prescription you ever write.

## Step 1 — Who's prescribing

Pick the **Attending Doctor**. Only staff marked as dentists appear.

**Diagnosis / CC** is optional — the complaint or diagnosis the prescription is for.

## Step 2 — Add the medication

Two ways, and the difference matters over time.

**Select from database** — pick a drug you've already set up. Dose and frequency come with it, so it's faster and it's spelled the same way every time.

**Manual entry** — type it yourself: drug name, dose / frequency, and optional special instructions.

Press **Add to Prescription**. The counter shows how many are on the prescription, and the preview updates as you go.

Add as many as you need — repeat the step for each.

> Use the database where you can. Hand-typed drug names drift — the same antibiotic spelled three ways can't be searched, counted, or checked against a patient's allergies later.

## Step 3 — Give it to the patient

Three buttons at the top:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Save** | Files it against the patient — it appears in their Prescriptions tab |
| **Print** | Prints it |
| **WhatsApp** | Sends the PDF to the patient's number |

**Save it even when you print or send.** Printing hands over paper; saving is what puts it in the record, so anyone opening the file later can see what this patient was given and when. That matters if they come back with a reaction, or ask for the same thing again.

## Allergies

The pink **Medical Alert** band on the patient's file lists their allergies. Nothing on this page stops you prescribing something they're allergic to — that check is still yours to make.

Which is the real reason the allergies box is worth filling in when the patient is first registered.
