The **Clinical** tab in a patient's file is their treatment history: what was done, to which tooth, by whom, and for how much.

## Where to find it

Open the patient, then the **Clinical** tab. It's the tab you land on.

![The clinical history timeline](/help/en/clinical-history.png)

## Reading the timeline

Each entry gives you, at a glance:

- **The procedure** — PFM Crown, Composite Filling, Extraction
- **Status** — Completed, and so on
- **Tooth** — the tooth number it was done to
- **The dentist** who did it
- **The cost**

Sorted **newest first** by default; the sort control flips it to oldest first when you want to read a course of treatment forwards.

## Adding a procedure

Press **Add New Procedure**.

Most of the time you don't need to — booking an appointment with a treatment already creates the clinical entry and the charge together. Use this when work was done that never had an appointment: something added mid-visit, or a walk-in treated on the spot.

> **A procedure added here still needs to reach the ledger to be charged.** Work recorded clinically but never billed is invisible money — the patient walks out owing nothing on a treatment they actually had. Check the patient's Finance tab afterwards and make sure the charge is there.

## Editing and deleting

Each row has an edit and a delete action.

Editing is the right tool for a wrong tooth number, a wrong cost, or a procedure logged against the wrong dentist.

**Deleting a clinical record is different from deleting most things.** It is a medical record: it says what was done to a person's body. Delete it only when it's genuinely wrong — logged on the wrong patient, or entered twice. If the treatment happened, it stays, even if the details need correcting.

Both are separate permissions, and neither comes with the Dentist role automatically — see *Roles and permissions*.

## Where the rest lives

The tabs across the top split the patient's file up:

| Tab | What's in it |
|---|---|
| **Clinical** | This timeline |
| **Finance** | Charges, payments, balance |
| **Timeline** | Visit history |
| **Overview** | Summary |
| **X-Rays & Photos** | Images |
| **Prescriptions** | Prescriptions written |

And the **Quick Actions** row — Write Rx, Diagnosis, Ortho — jumps to the three things a dentist reaches for mid-appointment.

## The medical alert

If the patient has allergies or a medical history recorded, a pink **Medical Alert** band sits across the top of their file — above everything else, in every tab.

That's why the allergy box matters when you create a patient. Filled in, it warns every dentist who ever opens the file. Left empty, nobody gets warned.
